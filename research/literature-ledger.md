# Literature and novelty ledger

Search and access date: 14 October 2026. This is a focused review, not a systematic
review, exhaustive patent search, or proof that no related work exists.

| Primary source | Role in this study | Boundary of the contribution |
|---|---|---|
| Rotunda, *Revolutionary Networks*, 2026, https://github.com/rotunda-uvap/revolutionary-networks | Original compilation and dictionary; frozen CSV at commit dacbbff257a769740c5bf8e25c6fb0a5212bf671 | Metadata dates and endpoints do not establish receipt, reading, or shared contents. |

| Ureña-Carrion et al., 2022, https://doi.org/10.1007/s41109-022-00463-1 | Large historical correspondence study, temporal statistics and logistical constraints | Historical communication timing is a studied field; this audit focuses on multi-document route admissibility. |

| Alassi & Rosenthaler, 2024, https://doi.org/10.1093/llc/fqae027 | RDF editions and layers of correspondence | Preserving source semantics has clear prior art. |
| An, 2026, https://doi.org/10.63744/AfxZkbfK4dXG | Historical temporal uncertainty in CIDOC CRM | Uncertain historical chronology is established; interval dating is not claimed as new. |
| Kane, 2024, https://www.jstor.org/stable/10.7591/jj.24541210.14 | Founders Online coverage and gendered archival formation | Missing routes do not identify absent historical relationships. Chapter pagination was checked against the open-access volume, pp. 184–200. |

Search families included historical correspondence temporal uncertainty, Founders
Online chronology and network analysis, Revolutionary Networks reachability,
minimum waiting temporal paths, and epistolary receipt/communication networks.
The review supports a **corpus-specific empirical and software contribution**,
not an unprecedented temporal-network theory. No directly matching analysis of
the frozen 2026 compilation was located in this focused search. That absence is
not a universal novelty guarantee.

The study is descriptive and was not preregistered. Corpus inspection preceded
the final analysis specification. Exclusion and date-permutation results are
explicitly exploratory. The illustrations use an explicit deterministic rule,
rather than being treated as representative historical cases. No transcript
content was analyzed; letter-level illustration claims refer only to metadata.

# Desk study of the software context

This is a comparison of documented product purposes, not evidence of purchasing
intent or a market-size estimate. It informs integration possibilities rather
than a claim that competing tools cannot implement the audit.

| Existing environment | Documented purpose | Plausible integration of DispatchGap |
|---|---|---|
| [Palladio, Stanford Humanities + Design](https://hdlab.stanford.edu/palladio/) | Tabular historical data, maps, graphs, lists, and galleries; MIT licensing | Source-linked route outputs can accompany visual relationships or exported lists. |
| [Gephi Desktop](https://gephi.org/desktop/) | Network exploration, visualization, statistics, filtering; open-source GPL software | Export audit results for exploration while retaining the documentary witness as a separate JSON record. |
| [nodegoat](https://nodegoat.net/) | Humanities data modelling, spatial and temporal visualization, network analysis, data publication and APIs | A route query could operate over a project's explicit data model and return provenance through an API. |

Institutional installations and mature open-source tools show that historical
data analysis has an existing software ecosystem. They do not establish demand
for a paid route-audit service. No customer interviews, paid-pilot evidence,
revenue forecast, or patentability conclusion is presented. A potential product
would need user testing with historians and archivists and a separate business
validation process.
